# Streaming a large browser world with no visible pop-in

Research pass for AI City (three.js r185, WebGL2, ~650 city blocks @ 32m + ~290 woods blocks, near/mid/far tiers, pre-bake in progress). Sources verified against primary repos/docs where possible; third-party summaries are flagged.

## 1. Scheduling

**Priority = error × frustum × distance × recency, not distance alone.** NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS (three.js, the closest browser-shipped analogue to a tiled/LOD world) evolved from a naive distance sort to: *"The default priority sort now accounts for most recently used tiles, screenspace error, and distance to the camera,"* and fixed a bug where *"tiles that were outside of the camera frustum would be loaded with a higher priority"* than they should — out-of-frustum work is explicitly deprioritized. [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md); originally proposed as a choice between `tile.__error` and `tile.__distance` as sort key ([issue #143](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/issues/143)).

**Per-frame scheduling, not immediate dispatch.** `PriorityQueue` was changed to *"wait a frame to schedule new tasks instead of using `Promise.resolve` so tasks will only be started once per frame"* — a hard per-frame gate on how much loading work starts. [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)

**Cancellation on camera move.** `PriorityQueue` supports *"passing an abort signal for an object,"* so in-flight requests for tiles that fall out of priority get cancelled rather than completing and being thrown away. [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)

**Hard memory caps, structured eviction.** *"Cache limits are hard caps. No new tiles load once the `LRUCache` reaches `maxSize` or `maxBytesSize`,"* and eviction *"unloads deepest tiles first, then least recently used."* [README](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/blob/master/README.md)

**Screen-space error as the loading trigger, not just render LOD.** Cesium (the reference implementation 3D Tiles/3DTilesRendererJS both target) defaults `maximumScreenSpaceError` to **16px**, adds `foveatedScreenSpaceError` (on by default, `foveatedConeSize=0.3`) to deprioritize peripheral-screen tiles vs. a center cone, `dynamicScreenSpaceError` (on by default) to relax required resolution toward the horizon, and `progressiveResolutionHeightFraction=0.3` to load a coarse pass across ~30% of screen height first. [Cesium3DTileset reference](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Cesium3DTileset.html)

**Small worker-budgeted chunk queues, verified in source.** `noa-engine` (Babylon.js, not three.js, but the engine bloxd.io is built on — confirmed in [noa's own README](https://github.com/fenomas/noa)) ships these defaults in `src/lib/world.js`: `chunkSize: 24`, `chunkAddDistance: [2, 2]`, `chunkRemoveDistance: [3, 3]`, `maxChunksPendingCreation: 50`, `maxChunksPendingMeshing: 50`, `maxProcessingPerTick: 5` (ms/tick on chunk work), `minNeighborsToMesh: 6` (won't mesh until enough neighbors exist, avoiding seam holes). Request order is driven by a swappable `chunkSortingDistFn` — direction-weighted prefetch is a supported hook, not a fork. [source](https://github.com/fenomas/noa/blob/master/src/lib/world.js)

**Budget-first design in an indie browser voxel project.** `st-voxel-action`'s streaming issue scopes itself around *"generation and meshing on workers, and a budget that keeps frame time stable while chunks arrive."* Their own frame-cost measurement found **terrain is 15% of a frame's draw, props are 62%** — why they deprioritized a terrain-only LOD pass in favor of prop culling. [issue #13](https://github.com/desphyxia/st-voxel-action/issues/13)

**GPU-driven cut instead of CPU distance checks.** `Trillion3D`, an independent WebGPU/WebGL2 "virtualized geometry for the web" project modeled on Nanite-class streaming, cuts a cluster DAG once per frame in compute using a per-cluster `lodScore`, under a **fixed** memory and frame-time budget (not auto-discovered per machine), loading "coarsest first, the rest drawn through ancestors" so there's always something to draw under pressure — the clearest browser-native transfer of GPU-driven LOD selection. [README](https://github.com/pasquelin/Trillion3D)

**Native transfer — grid + budgeted streaming.** Unreal's World Partition streams a persistent level as grid cells with a configurable loading range, and HLOD proxy meshes *"switch in/out based on a metric (such as loading range and OnScreenSize) to replace one or more source Actors."* [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition---hierarchical-level-of-detail-in-unreal-engine). A third-party deep-dive reports per-frame caps of `MaxStreamingCellsPerFrame=2`, `MaxActorsToSpawnPerFrame=10`, `MemoryBudgetMB=2048` — **unverified/third-party**. [StraySpark blog](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ue5-world-partition-deep-dive-streaming-hlod)

## 2. Hiding transitions

**Hysteresis via asymmetric add/remove radius.** noa's `chunkAddDistance:[2,2]` vs `chunkRemoveDistance:[3,3]` — with a minimum gap enforced in `setAddRemoveDistance` — means a chunk loads at radius 2 and only unloads past radius 3, so movement back and forth across one boundary can't thrash the load/unload queue. [source](https://github.com/fenomas/noa/blob/master/src/lib/world.js). three.js's own `THREE.LOD.addLevel(object, distance, hysteresis)` builds the identical idea in: *"hysteresis — threshold used to avoid flickering at LOD boundaries, as a fraction of distance"* (default 0, i.e. off unless set). [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/#api/en/objects/LOD)

**Dithered cross-fade instead of alpha blend.** 3DTilesRendererJS shipped a `TilesFadePlugin`, funded by a Cesium Ecosystem Grant to fix 3D-tile pop, which *"smooths the transition between tile levels of detail by modifying materials to add dither animation when fading in and out."* [Cesium blog](https://cesium.com/blog/2024/09/11/enhancements-to-the-threejs-3d-tiles-renderer/); a Babylon.js port references the existing three.js fade material manager directly. [PR #1745](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/pull/1745). The underlying idea — assign each fragment to one LOD via a noise threshold and discard, never alpha-blend — is documented natively by NVIDIA for stochastic LOD in ray tracing (~10% overhead, ~8 discrete steps from an 8-bit mask); dither beats blend because blending breaks depth/occlusion. The insight transfers directly to rasterized fragment-discard. [NVIDIA dev blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/implementing-stochastic-lod-with-microsoft-dxr)

**Load-in gate ("don't show until settled").** Cesium's `progressiveResolutionHeightFraction` shows a coarse pass immediately while resolving full detail in the background rather than popping a half-built tile into view. [Cesium3DTileset reference](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Cesium3DTileset.html) The general pattern — keep the currently-visible tier resident and swap only once the new tier is fully meshed/uploaded — is what the LRU hard-cap + deepest/least-recently-used eviction order in 3DTilesRendererJS is built to support (old tile isn't dropped just because a new one started loading). [README](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/blob/master/README.md)

## 3. Legibility at distance

**MSDF text instead of geometry-derived lettering.** Multi-channel signed-distance-field text renders crisp, correctly-cornered glyphs "whether you scale or rotate" by encoding distance-to-edge across three color channels (a single-channel SDF loses sharp corners; MSDF fixes that specifically). Shipping three.js utilities exist for this (`three-msdf-text-utils`, `three-bmfont-text`'s SDF doc). This is the direct fix for "lettering breaks at mid tier": letterforms baked as MSDF-textured decals stay legible independent of the merged-mesh simplification that's currently eating them. [three-msdf-text-utils](https://github.com/leochocolat/three-msdf-text-utils), [three-bmfont-text SDF doc](https://github.com/Jam3/three-bmfont-text/blob/master/docs/sdf.md), [SDF font primer](https://www.redblobgames.com/x/2403-distance-field-fonts/)

**Bake detail once, don't derive it from simplified geometry at mesh time.** Divine Voxel Engine's meshing is fast because "every culling and ambient-occlusion relationship is computed **once at registration** into flat lookup tables," so the runtime hot loop is array lookups, not per-face recomputation. [README](https://github.com/Divine-Star-Software/DivineVoxelEngine). Transferable idea: pre-bake legible sign/lettering geometry or decals as a distinct asset class rather than letting the mid-tier mesh merge derive it from voxels — aligns with the pre-bake step already underway.

**Impostor billboards at the farthest tier** are the standard native answer (Unreal HLOD's farthest layer, per a third-party breakdown — **unverified against Epic's own doc**, which confirms proxy meshes replace unloaded actors but not that the farthest layer is specifically billboards). [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition---hierarchical-level-of-detail-in-unreal-engine); [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ue5-world-partition-deep-dive-streaming-hlod)

## 5. Ranked changes for AI City

1. **Asymmetric load/unload radius per tier (hysteresis).** Load at radius N, unload at N+1 or N+2, so boundary-straddling movement can't re-trigger a swap. *Source: noa-engine `chunkAddDistance`/`chunkRemoveDistance`.* **Effort: S**
2. **Priority queue keyed on screen-space size × frustum × distance, with cancellation.** Stop treating "nearest first" as the whole policy; deprioritize/cancel work for blocks that left the frustum or shrank on screen. *Source: 3DTilesRendererJS CHANGELOG.* **Effort: M**
3. **Dithered cross-fade between tiers** (both tiers drawn opaque for ~0.3–0.5s, fragment discard against a screen-space noise/Bayer threshold) instead of a hard swap. Directly answers "blocks swap detail visibly." *Source: 3DTilesRendererJS TilesFadePlugin; NVIDIA stochastic LOD.* **Effort: M**
4. **Explicit per-tick ms budget for meshing/GPU upload**, not just a worker count — cap main-thread time spent absorbing finished chunks per frame so a burst entering view can't spike a frame. *Source: noa `maxProcessingPerTick=5`, `maxChunksPendingMeshing=50`.* **Effort: S–M**
5. **Direction-weighted prefetch.** Bias the load queue toward heading, not just Euclidean distance, using a swappable sort function. *Source: noa `chunkSortingDistFn`.* **Effort: S**
6. **Load-in gate: show far/massing immediately, swap to mid only once fully built.** Never let a partially-meshed tier be the first thing shown. *Source: Cesium `progressiveResolutionHeightFraction`; general "don't unload old until new is ready" practice in 3DTilesRendererJS's LRU design.* **Effort: S** (tiers already exist; this is sequencing)
7. **Bake mid-tier lettering as MSDF decals instead of deriving it from merged/simplified voxel geometry.** Directly answers "lettering breaks at mid tier"; folds into the pre-bake step already underway. *Source: three-msdf-text-utils; DivineVoxelEngine's bake-once-at-registration pattern.* **Effort: M**
8. **Hard memory ceiling with structured eviction (LRU + deepest-first), decoupled from view radius.** Stops evictor and prefetcher fighting each other when the player reverses direction. *Source: 3DTilesRendererJS LRUCache.* **Effort: M**
