# Crowds and traffic: rendering and managing pedestrians and cars in a browser city

## Continuity rules to adopt

The owner's two bugs — "pedestrians disappear when I move forward" and "some load, some don't" — both match well-documented failure modes in shipped open-world games, not exotic problems.

1. **Never create or destroy an entity the camera can see.** Every crowd system that avoids pop-in gates spawn/despawn on visibility, not just distance. GTA IV's own ped-pool culling is explicit about this: pedestrians past ~80 m are deleted immediately only if never "seen" by the camera; once flagged seen, deletion waits until ~115 m or until they go off-camera, with a 2.5–3.5 s grace timer before removal ([popctl reverse-engineering notes](https://github.com/gutbash/popctl)). Frustum culling (is it in the view cone) and occlusion culling (is something blocking it) are the two distinct tests engines use for this, and pop-in is explicitly what happens when an object appears without having been smoothly resolved by one of them first ([Unity occlusion culling manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/550/Documentation/Manual/OcclusionCulling.html)). If AI City's despawn ring is a simple radius around the player with no on-screen check, "pedestrians disappear in front of me" is the expected symptom, not a bug in the walking/turn-taking logic itself.
2. **Bias the spawn/despawn envelope toward travel direction, not a symmetric circle.** A ring centered on the player sweeps its trailing edge through on-screen space the instant the player advances. GTA IV's band-cull keeps a much longer camera-visible radius (up to 115 m) than its off-camera radius (70–80 m) for exactly this reason ([popctl](https://github.com/gutbash/popctl)).
3. **Add hysteresis to both LOD switches and the spawn/despawn boundary.** A separate "promote" and "demote" threshold (e.g., switch down at 105 m, back up only at 95 m) is the standard fix for flicker/pop at a single shared threshold ([Godot forum on LOD hysteresis](https://godotforums.org/d/17393-improved-level-of-detail-smooth-transitions-hysteresis); [DigitalRune LOD docs](https://digitalrune.github.io/DigitalRune-Documentation/html/b320aebd-46a0-45d8-8edb-0c717152a56b.htm)).
4. **Give instance pools headroom, and fail soft, not silently.** three.js `BatchedMesh`/`InstancedMesh` are allocated with a fixed `maxInstanceCount` at construction; instances beyond that count either don't render or need an explicit resize via `setInstanceCount()`/`setGeometrySize()` ([three.js BatchedMesh docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/BatchedMesh.html)). "Some pedestrians load, some don't" is the textbook symptom of a per-tier pool at its working peak.
5. **Decouple "simulated" agents from "rendered" crowd.** Assassin's Creed Unity's crowd GDC talk describes a pool system that swaps low-res/high-res NPC representations *without the player noticing*, capping real, individually-simulated AI at 40 and high-res models at 120, while up to 10,000 crowd NPCs are visible on screen at once ([GDC coverage](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/learn-about-i-assassin-s-creed-unity-i-s-dynamic-crowds-at-gdc-2015), [GDC Vault: "Massive Crowd on Assassin's Creed Unity: AI Recycling"](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022411/Massive-Crowd-on-Assassin-s)). This is the general pattern to copy: a small tracked-AI budget, a much larger renderer-only budget, and swaps that only happen out of view.

## Crowd-rendering approach for the three tiers

The existing near/mid/far split (GPU-skinned voxel bodies in one BatchedMesh → coarse bodies → boxes in one InstancedMesh) already matches the direction the industry has converged on for browser-scale crowds:

- **Near tier.** A single draw call for many GPU-animated characters is achievable in three.js today: a forum write-up of a pure three.js instanced-crowd system reports up to 100,000 characters in one `InstancedMesh` draw call, sub-2 ms GPU time and ~0.1 ms CPU time, using a shared merged geometry, packed per-instance attributes, and frame-amortized updates (not every character's transform updates every frame) ([three.js forum: "One Draw Call, Massive Crowd"](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/one-draw-call-massive-crowd-performance-engineering-in-three-js/89928)). `BatchedMesh` supports per-instance frustum culling (`perObjectFrustumCulled`) and per-instance visibility toggling (`setVisibleAt`) natively, the primitive the despawn-without-pop rule above needs ([three.js BatchedMesh docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/BatchedMesh.html)); one independent benchmark demoed 50,000 instances without FPS numbers, so treat that as a capacity bound, not a performance guarantee ([Wael Yasmina, BatchedMesh article](https://waelyasmina.net/articles/batchedmesh-for-high-performance-rendering-in-three-js/)). For animation cost, vertex animation textures (VAT) — baking a walk-cycle into a texture and sampling it per-instance in the vertex shader instead of CPU skinning — is the standard technique for large animated crowds; NVIDIA's GPU Gems 3 chapter reports ~10,000 independently-animated characters at 30 fps with effectively zero CPU cost per instance ([GPU Gems 3, Ch. 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-2-animated-crowd-rendering); [VAT/"texture animation" overview](https://medium.com/tech-at-wildlife-studios/texture-animation-techniques-1daecb316657)). Whether `BatchedMesh` cleanly carries the extra per-instance attribute (clip id/phase) VAT needs is unconfirmed here and should be spiked before replacing GPU skinning with it.
- **Mid tier.** The coarse-body approach is reasonable as-is. If it needs to get cheaper, octahedral/billboard impostors (pre-rendered sprite atlases from many angles, blended per-frame) are the established substitute for a full mesh at this range and are cited as far back as *Rome: Total War*'s battlefield crowds ([impostor/billboard overview](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2018/08/25/shader-showcase-saturday-7/); [academic survey: "Impostors, Pseudo-instancing and Image Maps for GPU Crowd Rendering"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220222206_Impostors_Pseudo-instancing_and_Image_Maps_for_GPU_Crowd_Rendering)) — but this is a nice-to-have, not something the current bug reports point to.
- **Far tier.** Boxes in one `InstancedMesh` are already close to the cheapest possible representation and match the same single-draw-call pattern the 100k-character demo above uses; no change indicated.

## Traffic design for one-way side streets and two-way avenues

**Side streets (4 m wide, two 2 m lanes, 1.8 m cars).** At 1.8 m cars in 2 m lanes, each lane leaves only ~0.2 m of clearance — not enough for a car to be legally overtaken or to change lanes without touching a neighbor. That geometry is effectively a single-file corridor per direction already, which is good news for cost: model each one-way block as a FIFO car-following queue rather than a full lane-change simulation. The **Intelligent Driver Model (IDM)** is the standard, well-validated car-following model for exactly this — acceleration is a function of gap-to-leader, relative speed, and desired speed, and it is collision-free by construction ([IDM, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_driver_model); [traffic-simulation.de, IDM detail](https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_IDM.html)). Red lights and stop lines are handled in IDM-based sims as a virtual stationary "vehicle" placed at the stop line, which the model naturally decelerates toward — traffic-simulation.de's open-source ramp/merge code (JS) implements merges and diverges this way and is a usable reference implementation ([movsim/traffic-simulation-de, README](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README.md)). No lane-change model is needed on these streets given the clearance math above.

**Avenues (two-way, presumably multi-lane).** Here lane changes are real, so pair IDM with **MOBIL** (Minimize Overall Braking Induced by Lane changes), a widely-used lane-change decision model that layers cleanly on top of IDM; a browser-based open-source implementation combining both exists in CoffeeScript/HTML5 with an interactive lane-graph editor (click to place intersections, drag to place roads) that is a reasonable structural reference for a lane-graph data model ([volkhin/RoadTrafficSimulator](https://github.com/volkhin/RoadTrafficSimulator)). For intersections and signals, simple fixed-cycle timing is sufficient at this scale — Cities: Skylines' much heavier agent-based system layers pathfinding cost (time/comfort/money weighted A*) on top of exactly this kind of local car-following behavior, but that routing sophistication is a later-stage concern, not a prerequisite ([how traffic works in Cities: Skylines](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-traffic-works-in-cities-skylines); [Cities: Skylines II traffic AI](https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Traffic)).

**Parking.** Given the current design already places static cars in lanes, the cheapest path is to keep parked cars as a simple per-cell occupancy state (occupied/free) that a moving car can claim on arrival and release on departure, rather than simulating a parking maneuver — this is a design inference, not sourced from a specific engine.

**Cost anchor.** IDM's per-vehicle update is a handful of arithmetic operations against its immediate leader, so CPU cost scales linearly and cheaply with vehicle count; no source here gives a browser-specific ceiling, so treat any number in this paragraph as an anchor, not a verified limit. GTA IV — a shipped, large open-world game — caps its actively-simulated vehicle pool at 140 stock, 150 hard ceiling ([popctl](https://github.com/gutbash/popctl)) — a reasonable order-of-magnitude target for a browser sim's "fully IDM-simulated" layer, with extra visual density from the same instanced/static representation already used for parked cars, not from full simulation.

## Ranked list of changes

1. **Gate despawn on visibility, not just radius** — never delete an on-screen or recently-seen pedestrian; this is the most likely direct fix for "they disappear in front of me" ([popctl](https://github.com/gutbash/popctl); [Unity culling docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/550/Documentation/Manual/OcclusionCulling.html)).
2. **Make the spawn/despawn envelope asymmetric toward travel direction** instead of a player-centered circle, so walking forward doesn't sweep the boundary through visible space ([popctl band-cull numbers](https://github.com/gutbash/popctl)).
3. **Audit and add headroom to per-tier instance pools** (`BatchedMesh`/`InstancedMesh` `maxInstanceCount`), with a resize-not-drop failure mode — likely fix for "some load, some don't" ([three.js BatchedMesh docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/BatchedMesh.html)).
4. **Add hysteresis bands to both LOD tier switches and the spawn/despawn threshold** to kill any remaining flicker at the boundary ([Godot LOD hysteresis thread](https://godotforums.org/d/17393-improved-level-of-detail-smooth-transitions-hysteresis)).
5. **Split "simulated" from "rendered" population**: cap fully-tracked/pathing agents (sim's own AI) at a small number, recycle a much larger visual-only crowd pool, and only swap detail levels off-camera, mirroring ACU's 40-real/120-high-res/10,000-visible split ([GDC coverage](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/learn-about-i-assassin-s-creed-unity-i-s-dynamic-crowds-at-gdc-2015)).
6. **Model one-way side streets as single-file IDM queues** (no lane-change model needed given the 0.2 m lane clearance), using a virtual-stopped-vehicle trick at red lights ([IDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_driver_model); [traffic-simulation-de source](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README.md)).
7. **Reserve IDM+MOBIL full simulation for avenues only**, fixed-cycle signals, budgeted to roughly the same order of magnitude as GTA IV's ~140-vehicle active pool, with everything else static/instanced as today ([RoadTrafficSimulator](https://github.com/volkhin/RoadTrafficSimulator); [popctl vehicle pool numbers](https://github.com/gutbash/popctl)).
8. **(Lower priority) Spike VAT for the near tier** to move walk-cycle animation off the CPU entirely, and/or swap mid-tier coarse bodies for baked impostor sprites — worth doing once the continuity bugs above are fixed, not before ([GPU Gems 3 Ch. 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-2-animated-crowd-rendering); [impostor survey](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220222206_Impostors_Pseudo-instancing_and_Image_Maps_for_GPU_Crowd_Rendering)).
