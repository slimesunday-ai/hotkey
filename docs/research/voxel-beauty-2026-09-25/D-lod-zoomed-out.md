# D — Zoomed-out detail: LOD that keeps the style

Scope: why the city reads as generic low-poly from the default overhead camera, and how to fix it without an engine rewrite. Code grounding against `world/`. Camera: `main.js` sets `controls.minDistance = 6; controls.maxDistance = 320;` — a bounded, human-scale city view, not an open-world draw-distance problem.

## TL;DR

- **The "generic from overhead" complaint has a precise, verified cause.** Far-tier homes are literal flat single-color boxes with zero texture (`kit/assets/modules.js` `makeFar`, home branch passes raw color `f.wall` straight into `box()`, never through `texture()`). Far-tier towers keep the tiled brick/plaster material on the wall shell, but windows are flat panels chosen by independent per-cell noise (`districts/landmarks.js`, `makeTower`, `!detail` branch) with no clustering and no frame geometry — the literal source of "reads as noise." [verified, code read]
- **The ~170-frame invisible-building bug (problem 8) is a real, reproducible code path**, not a one-off glitch. Both LOD systems gate new template/block builds behind a per-frame deadline, and when an object has never been shown before, the code just `continue`s — drawing nothing — until the budget catches up. Confirmed in two places: `integration/viewer.js:107` and `districts/viewer.js:26`. [verified, code read]
- **Near-detail is hard-rationed to a handful of blocks citywide.** `districts/viewer.js` defaults `nearLimit=6`; `main.js:67-70` sets it to 6/4/6/2 across block types, each also gated to within 100 units of its district's focus point. From a wide establishing shot, the large majority of visible blocks default to the flat far tier regardless of visual prominence. [verified, code read]
- **Anti-flicker hysteresis is already implemented three separate ways** (a flat +10m buffer, a -12 "sticky" score bonus, a ×1.05/0.95 threshold band). This part needs its far-tier *content* fixed, not new switching logic.
- **AAA precedent treats "make the far geometry decent" as the fix, and atmosphere as a finishing touch, not a cover-up.** GTA V explicitly uses fog to hide low-poly distant buildings (Adrian Courrèges' graphics study) — honest about being a crutch. AI City's fog is doing the same job for a far tier that isn't good yet, which is why haze reads as "swallowing" districts (problem 5) instead of adding depth.
- **Full billboard/impostor systems are the wrong tool for this camera.** Spider-Man's IG-Impostor system exists for a free camera viewing Manhattan near-horizontally at speed; AI City's camera orbits from overhead and looks down on rooftops, and a flat card can't fake a roof from above. Voxel-terrain LOD mods (Distant Horizons, Voxy) are also wrong here: they solve unlimited natural-terrain draw distance, not a ~320-unit-max city of authored assets.
- **The two Shadows of Doubt devblogs named in the shared brief don't cover distant/LOD rendering** — verified by reading both. DevBlog 21 covers the voxel asset-authoring pipeline; DevBlog 8 covers citizen/economy simulation. Flagging so other agents don't re-cite them here.
- **The single highest-leverage fix is cheap and low-risk**: route the far-tier home wall color through the same `texture()` call the near tier already uses.

## Findings by sub-question

### 1. Techniques

**HLOD / merged proxies.** AI City already has a form of this: `performance/batch.js` merges static geometry inside each **64 m cell** into one mesh once stationary for 250 ms (`createBatcher(scene, root, {size=64, still=250})`, via `BufferGeometryUtils.mergeGeometries`). That's a *draw-call* optimization (same triangles, fewer meshes), not a *visual-complexity* reduction — it doesn't touch the flat-box problem, it just makes many flat boxes cheaper to draw. Right layer for performance, wrong layer for cohesion.

**Impostors (billboard / octahedral).** An octahedral impostor bakes a mesh into a direction atlas, then at runtime blends the nearest 2-3 atlas cells by view direction — cheap and convincing for a roughly fixed elevation band. [likely: https://github.com/ektogamat/octahedral-impostor-component, https://www.alanzucconi.com/2018/08/25/shader-showcase-saturday-7/] The failure mode is exactly AI City's camera: a **top-down** view needs the roof baked into the atlas too, which stops being cheap at city-block scale, and a flat card still can't give correct parallax between a tower's near and far faces. A tool for trees/crowds viewed near-horizontally, not hero buildings under an orbit camera.

**Baking near detail into far textures.** Cities: Skylines does this at asset-import time: auto-bakes the high-poly near model onto the low-poly LOD mesh's UVs, with all LOD textures per category (buildings/props/vehicles/nets) sharing one 4096×4096 atlas. [likely: https://cslmodding.info/asset/building/] The AAA-adjacent version of "give the far box a real texture instead of a flat color": render each unique near kit template from a few fixed angles to an offscreen target once at build time, use that as the far box's material. A legitimate stretch goal (see Apply), more powerful than a flat re-texture but real engineering effort.

**Simplified voxel LODs.** Mikola Lysenko's POP-buffer method for blocky voxel terrain sorts quads by LOD via "round coordinates down to the previous power of two," so one buffer implicitly encodes every coarser level, and **geomorphing** (interpolating vertices between levels) removes the need for seam skirts between chunks of different detail. [verified: https://0fps.net/2018/03/03/a-level-of-detail-method-for-blocky-voxels/] Its own stated tradeoff: vertex rounding keeps the blocky silhouette but makes thin features — exactly what AI City's kit is praised for (fire escapes, awnings, railings) — vanish abruptly. Built for natural, high-count terrain voxels, not a few thousand hand-authored buildings — not a fit for AI City's asset model, though the geomorphing idea (interpolate, don't hard-cut) is portable to the discrete near/far swap already in place.

**Procedural facade shaders.** Canonical reference: Müller & Wonka's CGA shape grammar (basis of Esri CityEngine) — generate a building mass, then recursively subdivide facades into floors/bays/windows via context-sensitive rules. [likely: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1141911.1141931] A cheaper fragment-shader cousin defines the facade as a UV-space grid evaluated per-pixel, no extra geometry. [likely, general literature] AI City's far-tower window loop is already a primitive version of this — a grid of bays × floors, procedurally lit/unlit — it's just raw independent noise per cell instead of a grammar or lower-frequency pattern, which is why it reads as noise instead of rhythm. One concrete idea from a related patent: vary the lit-window function by **floor band and building index together**, not per cell, so neighboring floors/buildings don't read as uniform static. [likely, not independently confirmed]

**Silhouette-preserving simplification.** Worth stating as a positive: `makeTower`'s `!detail` branch still loops every tier (`b.tiers`) at its own width/depth/height, so setbacks and stepped massing survive into the far tier, and the home far-tier (`makeFar`) keeps a simplified pitched roof (`simpleRoof`) rather than collapsing to a plain box. Silhouette logic isn't the weak link — surface treatment is.

### 3. Transitions and pop-in

`THREE.LOD` already bakes in the standard toolkit: `addLevel(object, distance, hysteresis)` where `hysteresis` is "a fraction of distance" that prevents boundary flicker, plus `update(camera)`/`autoUpdate`. [verified: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LOD.html] AI City doesn't use it — it hand-rolls the equivalent three times over (see TL;DR), and the hand-rolled version already does more than the class offers: per-frame build-time budgeting (`deadline`) and InstancedMesh batching per (template, detail) pair. **Migrating to `THREE.LOD` would be a step backward.**

Dithered cross-fade (Unity's LOD Group, Cesium for Unreal): render both levels briefly, threshold a screen-space noise/Bayer pattern against a ramping alpha so old and new tiles are complementary — no gap, no overdraw — ideally with TAA so the noise's temporal accumulation reads as a blend. [likely: https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/lod/lod-transitions-lod-group.html, https://cesium.com/blog/2022/10/20/smoother-lod-transitions-in-cesium-for-unreal/] More machinery than AI City needs: near/far geometry here differs enough (a deli awning appearing vs. a flat wall) that a cross-fade would look like double-exposure, not a blend. GTA V and Cities: Skylines both just hard-cut with hysteresis — what AI City already does.

What actually matters is **keep the old version visible until the new one is ready**, and AI City implements it *inconsistently*. `districts/viewer.js` gets it right for *re-builds* (comment at line 23: "A block changing detail keeps its current version on screen until the replacement is built" — the old object stays in the scene until a replacement is built and swapped in, line 27). Both `districts/viewer.js:26` and `integration/viewer.js:107` get **first appearance** wrong: with nothing ever shown yet, there's no fallback, so a deadline miss means the code `continue`s and draws nothing — the mechanism behind problem 8. Being deadline-budget-driven, it plausibly recurs in normal play under load too, not only in the background-tab test — but I did not run the game to confirm frame counts carry over to normal foreground ticking.

Prioritizing by what the camera is looking at is only partly implemented: `districts/viewer.js`'s `score()` ranks near-detail candidates purely by 2D distance to a fixed per-district target — it doesn't know where the camera is pointed, so an edge-of-screen block and a dead-center block at the same distance score identically.

### 4. Budget

AI City already streams and instances well: texture-array block tiles for terrain, `InstancedMesh` per (template, detail) pair for kit assets (`integration/viewer.js:121`), and per-frame build-time deadlines in both LOD systems. What it lacks is anything **screen-space-error-driven** — budget is allocated by raw world-space distance and fixed head-counts (`nearLimit`), never by pixel coverage or frame position. The general principle — spend budget where projected size/view-centrality is highest, not just where world-distance is smallest — comes from classic view-dependent terrain LOD literature (Hoppe's progressive meshes: https://hhoppe.com/svdlod.pdf) [likely]; the exact algorithm is for continuous meshes and doesn't transfer, but the resource-allocation idea does — `districts/viewer.js`'s `score()` is the one place to fold in "near screen-center" as a cheap multiplier.

Three.js tools, current as of the version pinned here (`three@0.185.1`, `index.html:100`):
- **`InstancedMesh`** — in use; homogeneous geometry, cheap.
- **`BatchedMesh`** — not in use. Renders many different geometries under one material in one draw call, with `setGeometryIdAt()` to swap an instance's geometry live. [verified: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/BatchedMesh.html] Could collapse near/far switching into one call instead of moving entities between InstancedMesh pools, but has **no native LOD/hysteresis** — needs a third-party extension (`@three.ez/batched-mesh-extensions`). [verified re: no native support; likely re: extension features] Adds a dependency for a problem the current approach already solves — low priority.
- **Texture arrays / KTX2** — already used for terrain tiles; the same array could hold baked far-facade textures with no new loader infrastructure. `KTX2Loader` (Basis Universal transcode) ships in three.js addons. [likely, not re-fetched this session: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/KTX2Loader.html]
- **WebGL2 limits** — `MAX_ARRAY_TEXTURE_LAYERS` guaranteed ≥256 under the GLES 3.0 baseline. [likely, reasoned from spec minimum, not re-read from the Khronos registry this session] Not binding at this scale.
- **WebGPU** — three.js's `WebGPURenderer` is reportedly production-ready, with continued WebGPU work in recent releases. [anecdotal/likely — third-party blogs; I hit inconsistent dates trying to pin exact timing this session, so treat "when" as unverified] Irrelevant to the immediate fix: this project runs `WebGLRenderer`, and none of the recommendations below require moving off it.

Per-district budgets exist as `nearLimit` in `main.js:67-70` (6/4/6/2) — a flat guess, not tuned to the locked art direction's downtown-vs-trailer-park hierarchy (see Apply).

### 5. Cohesion across LODs

This is the crux of slime's "mechanical" verdict, and the code confirms it precisely: **cohesion isn't broken by having two tiers, it's broken by what the far tier is made of.** The near tier, the far tower shells, and the coarse voxel trees share one grammar — chunky primitives with a tiled per-block texture. The far *home* tier breaks that grammar completely (flat solid color, no texture pass), and far *tower windows* break it partially (flat color by noise, no texture or pattern). Everywhere the far tier still calls `texture(...)` (tower wall shells) reads fine in the screenshots — it's specifically the untextured flat surfaces that read as "smooth boxes" next to the coarse voxel trees and chunky characters (problem 4). Going by the screenshots alone (the original's source wasn't in scope here, so this is inference, not a code-read), its charm at any distance is consistent with either no separate far tier or one that never dropped the per-block texture pass — "one handmade object at every distance" is the same description the brief gives for its close-up look. Veloren's "re-voxelize the LOD stand-in" move (above) is the same insight from an unrelated codebase: a simplified representation has to go back through the same stylization pass as the detailed one, or it stops reading as the same material.

## Apply to AI City

Ordered by leverage (impact ÷ effort), each mapped to the numbered problems from the shared brief.

1. **Fix the first-appearance skip (problem 8).** In `integration/viewer.js` (~line 107) and `districts/viewer.js` (~line 26): when there's no previously-shown version to fall back to and the deadline is exceeded, don't `continue` — force-build the **far** tier synchronously for that entity regardless of deadline (it's the cheap tier by construction) instead of skipping the draw entirely. **Effort: S. Cost:** negligible, fires only on true first-appearance. **Risk: none** — pure bugfix.

2. **Route far-tier home walls through `texture()` instead of a flat color (problems 1, 4).** In `kit/assets/modules.js`, `makeFar`'s home/room branch passes `f.wall` (raw color) directly to `box()`; wrap it the way `buildNear` already does (`texture(f.surface, f.wall, …)`), coarser tiling if needed. **Effort: S** (one call site). **Cost:** near-zero — templates are cached (`kit/index.js:56,60`, refcount-evicted), so this is a one-time cost per unique far template, and it reuses a texture already resident for the near tier. **Risk: none — the single most direct fix for "reads as generic low-poly, not voxel."**

3. **De-noise the far tower window pattern (problem 3).** In `districts/landmarks.js`, `makeTower`'s `!detail` branch: replace the independent per-cell `noise(...)>.68` threshold with a banded pattern (quantize by floor-group, or coarser 2D noise across floor×bay instead of a per-cell hash) so lit windows cluster instead of scattering like static; optionally add one thin inset border per bay so panels read as windows. **Effort: S/M. Cost:** negligible. **Risk: none.**

4. **Raise and re-prioritize the near-detail budget (problems 1, 4).** `nearLimit` (6/4/6/2, `main.js:67-70`) is a flat, purely-distance-ranked cap. Raise it (needs an in-engine profiling pass I couldn't run — treat as "worth testing," not a promised win), and fold view-centrality into `districts/viewer.js`'s `score()` alongside raw distance so blocks the camera is actually pointed at outrank equidistant edge-of-frame blocks. **Effort: M. Cost:** more near-tier geometry live at once, bounded by the 64 m-cell batcher — needs in-engine measurement. **Risk: low**, same visual language, more of it.

5. **Retune fog only after items 2–4 land (problem 5).** Once far buildings carry real texture/pattern, re-check `scene.fog` bounds (`new THREE.Fog(0xcfdbe6, 110, SMALL?180:330)`, `main.js`) — the goal is atmospheric depth over acceptable geometry (GTA V's model), not concealment over bad geometry (today's "haze swallows the port and far suburbs"). **Effort: S. Cost:** none. **Risk: low, but sequence-dependent** — retuning fog first just exposes the flat boxes more.

6. **Stretch: bake near-template renders into far-tier facade textures (problems 1, 4; higher ceiling than #2/#3).** Cities: Skylines' approach — render each unique near kit template from a few fixed angles to an offscreen target once at build time, store as a small (optionally KTX2) texture, use it on the far box instead of the procedural surface. Captures real facade richness (awnings, fire escapes) cheaply at runtime. **Effort: L** — offscreen render pass, UV setup, cache/eviction on top of the existing template cache. **Cost:** one-time bake per unique template, modest new texture memory. **Risk: low, but the highest-effort item here — only if #2/#3 don't get the city far enough.** Don't start here.

7. **Later, only if draw calls become the bottleneck: evaluate `BatchedMesh` with a third-party LOD extension.** Not urgent — the current InstancedMesh-per-(template,detail) approach already works. **Effort: M. Risk:** adds a dependency for marginal gain today.

## What not to do

- **Don't build a full 3D impostor/persistent-cache system like Spider-Man's IG-Impostors.** Solves open-world, kilometers-of-skyline, near-horizontal-camera problems AI City doesn't have at a 320-unit max camera distance.
- **Don't adopt billboard or octahedral impostors for buildings.** The orbit camera looks straight down on rooftops; a flat card has no correct top-down silhouette. Fine for trees/crowds viewed near-horizontally, wrong for hero buildings.
- **Don't port a voxel-terrain LOD mod's approach wholesale.** Distant Horizons/Voxy-style runtime mesh simplification solves unbounded natural-terrain draw distance with millions of near-identical voxels; AI City has a few thousand hand-authored assets in a bounded map — the lever is better-textured far meshes, not a generalized voxel simplifier.
- **Don't migrate the hand-rolled LOD logic to `THREE.LOD`.** It already does what that class offers plus what it doesn't (frame-budgeted async builds, InstancedMesh batching). Pure churn.
- **Don't lean harder on fog/haze as the primary fix.** Already over-relied-on per problem 5; more of it makes "big empty haze" worse until the geometry underneath is worth revealing.
- **Don't do the render-to-texture bake (#6) before the cheap fixes (#2/#3).** Highest-effort item here, may not even be needed.
