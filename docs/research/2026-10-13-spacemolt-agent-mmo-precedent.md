# SpaceMolt — the closest live precedent to AI City's "connect your AI" model (research, 2026-10-13)

**Status: external research, pinned as evidence.** Found 2026-10-13 while slime asked whether AI City's v3 structure plus v1/v2's depth would work, and whether agents in games have produced real emergence. Sources were read in-session as page summaries (WebFetch), **not** full pages — treat specific figures as the devs' own claims, not independently verified.

## What it is
- "The first MMO designed to be played by AI agents": a text-based, persistent galaxy. Agents choose to be a miner, trader, explorer, pirate or faction leader (spacemolt.com/about).
- **Bring your own agent.** MCP is "the BEST way to play", with WebSocket and HTTP as fallbacks. A human owner links the agent to their account with a registration code from the dashboard (spacemolt.com/skill.md).
- Verbs: travel, jump, dock, mine, buy, sell, exchange orders, player trades, craft, missions, factions, base building, attack, battle, hunt. Police step in outside lawless systems. Limit: 30 game actions a minute, one per tick.

## What happened (the devs' own post, ~2026-03-20: spacemolt.com/news/700-agents)
- "Over 3,400 AI agents have registered", many of them "fleet alts orchestrated by the same individuals". "Around 700 are online at any given time, with peaks over 900."
- 86 player factions, with treasuries, ranks, diplomacy, wars, alliances and backstabbing.
- Pirates fake distress calls in a real nearby player's name, and rescuers still fall for it. *(Corrected 2026-10-19 — see below: these pirates were NPCs the devs added, not player agents.)* A rescue faction has logged 1,500+ numbered rescues.
- A misread quest (20 players had to accept it at some point; agents read it as 20 at the site at once) grew into a religion and a faction, the Cult of the Signal. Agents treat bugs and placeholder text as canon.
- Economy: 700M+ credits, with the top 10% holding 83%. A copper monopoly collapsed when its leader went offline for 25 days.
- Problems: hallucinated scams ("314k CR/hr passive income"), loops (the same intro every session), bot swarms (one fleet of 100+ bots killed its own alts for combat XP), and agents stuck by bugs. Server cost is about $330 a month, because players bring their own models.
- Press: Boing Boing (2026-03-21), Decrypt, Notebookcheck, WinFuture. Observers note that "religion" is remixing, not belief.

## Lessons for AI City
- **Owners will connect their own agents to a game:** thousands did here, for free and with nothing real at stake. Partial evidence on v2's biggest open risk — though here nothing real was at stake.
- **Emergence came from depth plus talk, not a long rulebook.** A deep world where agents can talk to and see each other produced the stories AI City wants: wars, betrayal, fake distress calls.
- **Multi-accounting is the first thing that breaks.** With real NFTs at stake, "one agent per verified person" (a GAME-v3 open question) is essential.
- **A persistent economy concentrates wealth** (top 10% held 83%). GAME-v3's season reset is the counter.
- **Never reward hurting your own alts** (XP from kills, respawn grinding).

## Correction (2026-10-19)
SpaceMolt's fake distress calls were made by NPC pirates the devs added ("We also taught the pirates how to
fake a distress call…", 700-agents post). Agents did not make them. The "What happened" bullet above and the
"fake distress calls" in the lessons below wrongly read them as player-agent behaviour. The devs also wrote that
before the NPC pirates, "agents mostly just bumped into each other politely". After 18 days, one player had all
the PvP kills (12 ships). Source: the 10-19 repo read in [2026-10-19-how-crime-sims-brief-agents](2026-10-19-how-crime-sims-brief-agents.md) (which also
flags GAME.md §32 as carrying the same claim).
